Understanding PS's Data's Sensitivity Profile 

What types of data the tool will encounter and why each type presents different risks. 

PS will process three distinct categories of data, each with different security implications 

●  First, there are the prompts themselves, which could contain anything from harmless test 

queries to proprietary business logic, customer data, or even trade secrets. 

( e.g A developer accidentally scans a prompt containing "Our new product launch strategy 
targets customers earning over $150,000 in the healthcare sector" - that's competitive 
intelligence that could damage the company if leaked.) 

●  Second, you're collecting metadata about scanning patterns, which might seem 

innocuous but can reveal significant information about an organization's AI usage, 
security concerns, and operational patterns. 

(e.g If someone could see that a company suddenly started scanning for financial compliance 
violations every Friday afternoon, they might infer something about the company's regulatory 
challenges or business cycles.) 

●  Third, the scan results themselves create a permanent record of what security issues 

were detected, when, and how the system responded. 

(e.g This audit trail is valuable for compliance but also represents a concentrated collection of 
your organization's security vulnerabilities and response patterns.)
